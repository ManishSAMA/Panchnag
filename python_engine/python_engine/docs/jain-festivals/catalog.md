# Shwetambar Jain Festival Catalog v1.0.0

1. **Shwetambar Murtipujak Tapagachchha** (Default)
2. **Shwetambar Sthanakvasi**
3. **Shwetambar Terapanthi**

## Detailed Rule Clarifications

### 1. Paryushan and Samvatsari Tithi Bisection
Paryushan is calculated as an 8-day block ending on the Samvatsari date.
* **Tapagachchha Samvatsari**: Bhadrapada Shukla 4.
* **Sthanakvasi / Terapanthi Samvatsari**: Bhadrapada Shukla 5.
* **Commencement**:
  * Tapagachchha: Bhadrapada Krishna 12 (or Shravana Krishna 12 in Amanta scheme).
  * Sthanakvasi / Terapanthi: Bhadrapada Krishna 13 (or Shravana Krishna 13 in Amanta scheme).
* **Source Conflicts**: During years where there are differing local calendar calculations (commonly known as "Be Tithi" conflicts where some sanghs celebrate on Chaturthi and others on Panchami), the engine will output a status of `review_needed` for the affected date, keep both candidate dates in the metadata, and trigger warning styles in the UI.

### 2. Ayambil Oli Ranges
Ayambil Oli is a strict 9-day fasting observance held twice a year (Chaitra and Ashvin) from Shukla 7 to Shukla 15 (Purnima).
* The engine resolves this by generating the 9 daily snapshots starting on Shukla 7 and continuing through Shukla 15.
* If a Tithi is duplicated (Vriddhi), the fast continues for the duplicated day (resulting in a 10-day physical period, but spiritually 9 parts).
* If a Tithi is skipped (Kshaya), the engine adjusts to ensure that exactly 9 days of fasts are observed, marking the transition correctly.

### 3. Tithi Vriddhi and Kshaya Handling
* **Tithi Vriddhi (Duplicate)**: A Tithi is active at two consecutive sunrises. Unless specified otherwise by a custom festival rule, the festival is observed on the **first day** (for kalyanaks/festivals) or the **second day** (if specific to Tapagachchha Samvatsari duplicate rules).
* **Tithi Kshaya (Skipped)**: A Tithi starts after a sunrise and ends before the next sunrise, meaning it is never the Udaya Tithi. In such cases, the festival is observed on the **next day** (when the next Tithi starts at sunrise), ensuring no event is missed.

### 4. Adhika Month Handling
* When an Adhika (intercalary) month occurs (e.g., Adhika Bhadrapada), the festival is celebrated during the **Nija (normal) month**.
* The Adhika month itself is labeled with the "Adhika" prefix and is not matched against standard festival dates, avoiding incorrect double celebrations.

### 5. Location-Awareness
* All festivals are calculated dynamically based on local **Udaya Tithi** (the Tithi prevailing at the local coordinates at sunrise + 2h24m reference).
* Therefore, the Gregorian date of a festival can occasionally shift by one day depending on the user's selected location, which is mathematically calculated and authentic to Agamic principles.
